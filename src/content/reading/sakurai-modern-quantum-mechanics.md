Most introductions start with the Schrödinger equation and treat spin as a complication added
later. Sakurai inverts this: he opens with Stern–Gerlach, a two-state system, and builds the
whole formalism from the smallest possible Hilbert space.

## Why the ordering matters

By the time wave mechanics appears, you already have state vectors, operators, and measurement.
The wavefunction $\psi(x) = \braket{x}{\psi}$ arrives as a *representation* — a projection onto
a particular basis — rather than as the fundamental object.

> The physical state is the ket. The wavefunction is a set of coordinates for it.

That reframing is hard to get from a book that introduces $\psi$ first, and it pays off in every
later chapter.

## Notes to self

- Chapter 1 rewards being read twice. Chapter 3 rewards being read three times.
- The problems are essential, not optional; the text quietly assumes several results that only
  appear in them.
